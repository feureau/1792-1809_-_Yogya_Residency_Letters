### 1793 Loano / Pacitan Report 3 (Van IJsseldijk)

*(Dj. Br. 45: W. van Ysseldijk, 'Eerbiedig Bericht')*

#### Bay of Pacitan & Maritime Considerations:

- **Bay of Pacitan**: ½ an hour's journey across; hemmed in by mountains on both sides; completely open to south wind.
- Feasibility of coastal shipping: Possible to use boats to load pepper, but significant **danger of pirates** using large two-masted brigs $\therefore$ safer to transport pepper overland.
- *Geopolitical sensitivity:* If V.O.C. conducts a survey of the Bay, it would arouse acute suspicion in the Sultan and Sunan that the Company intends to seize it.

---

#### Tree Counts & Expected Yields:

- **Loano**: 8,000 old pepper trees $\rightarrow$ only produce 70–80 pikuls per annum.
- **Pacitan**: 10,000 trees in much more flourishing condition; inhabitants are better able to tend them.
- Immediate harvest expectations:
  - 300 pikuls from Yogya Pacitan.
  - 370 pikuls from Solo Pacitan.
- **Total Lands Suitable**:
  - Pacitan / Loano: **800 cacahs (192 jungs)**.
  - Room for 1,000,000 pepper vines in Loano.
  - Additional 144 jungs across both districts could likely be brought into cultivation (60 square roods = 1 jung).
  - Cultivation techniques: Gardens surrounded by low walls; planting of **dadap trees** to provide shade for over 100,000 pepper bushes.
  - Well maintained in Pacitan; 60,000–70,000 new cuttings expected annually.

---

#### Official Decrees & Letters from Danureja I (DRI):

- **22 Sept. 1792**: Danureja I to *Demang* Gajah Kèmitèr and *Demang* Mangundiwiria:
  - Loano / Malarang freed from all royal corvée services and reserved exclusively for pepper under **Tumenggung Mangundipura**.
  - Strict injunction: local villagers must not be oppressed.
- **28 Jan. 1793 (15 Jumadilakir Dal 1719)**: Danureja I to Resident Van IJsseldijk:
  - For Pepper & Indigo: **700 cacahs** in Loano, Nelotiyang, Pekalongan, Malarang, Plaosan, and Winong (*+ 200 cacahs in Pacitan*) ceded to V.O.C.
  - HB II exempts all cultivators from customary corvée duties so that they can concentrate entirely on crops.
