### Part I: HB II to Rēksanēgara — Tollgate / Customs Dues (1 Sawal 1725 / 8 March 1799)

- *Pathoolen* [patola cloths], gold thread, spun silk, tea, *klonus* sugar: to be delivered as presents and payment of tax at *Mulud* and *Puasa* (total value: **1,230 Reals**).
  - **Pathoolen types:**
    - 10 pieces *cindhe nelamprang*, each @ 25 Reals.
    - 10 pieces *kanigara*, each @ 15 Reals.
    - Dry rot / pattern *kasonga*.
  - **Gold thread:** 10 lbs, each of 12 *dypen* (strands), @ 24 Reals per lb.
  - **Spun silk:** 20 *kaces* (kati) (9 *kaces* green, 2 *kaces* rose-red) @ 8 Reals per *kacé*.
  - **Tea & klonus sugar:** alone 430 Reals per half year.
- **Patompo / administrative fees:**
  - 10 Reals per 1,000 Reals of tax.
  - 56 Reals for purchase of receipt.
  - 28 Reals *Penglamur*.
- **Toll Exemption:** If villagers bring *kurdjes* / tobacco as presents for their Lord, **no tolls could be levied**.

### Part III: Inheritance of Mangkunegara I and Resident Van Reede

[Red heading: **Inheritance of MN I & Van Rheede.**]

*(Sources: W.H. van Vogelaar, Servatius, J.M. Rosenwinckel 1802; report to Daendels n.d. 1809)*

- On the death of Resident Van Reede, the administration of the inheritance was taken over by A.H. Wiese and P. Walbeek.
- **Van Reede destroyed all evidence** relating to the claims of PWD (MN II) on the estate of MN I.
- PWD was not allowed to succeed to his grandfather’s inheritance except through the good offices of Nicolaas Engelhard, 1½ years after Van Reede’s death.
- PWD had originally owed his appointment in 1795 to the good offices of Van Reede.
- Van Reede made no secret of seizing parts of the Mangkunegaran inheritance; he had maintained close terms with MN I.
- Van Reede brought his bodyguard and scribe Michaelis to MN I’s dalem at his deathbed.
- The dalem was crowded at death with wives and personal followers.
- **PWD did not accompany the body of his grandfather to the royal graves** [noted: *Imagiri?* crossed/corrected in red to **Měngadêg**]; he stayed in the dalem to prevent the inheritance from being looted and to guard against an attack by Pangeran Mangkubumi, who had designs on PWD’s life.
- PWD helped carry chests of treasure to Van Reede’s residency house — Van Reede claimed this was on the pretext of preventing them from falling into the hands of PB IV and his brothers.
