```
from                                            CHRIST'S COLLEGE
SIMON SCHAMA                                    CAMBRIDGE
Director of Studies in History                  CB2 3BU
                                                Cambridge 65454

                                                March 22nd 1976

Dear Peter,

    Thanks very much for the felicitations. As you know I really
didn't expect to get the job so it all came as an extraordinary
surprise. I'm hugely looking forward to coming to Oxford even though
I'm fully briefed as to the agonies as well as the ecstasies. I hear
from Richard that congrats are in order for you too for getting that
plum award from the BA to go East. Lucky s.o.b. - I'm green with envy
at all that lovely research time. Roll on the first sabbatical.

    I do feel terrible about that balls-up I made in Cambridge -
how can you ever forgive me. I was so discombobulated what with the job
news and the sudden and rather dramatic crisis it had plunged Christ's
history into that everything else got swept clean away - milles salaams.
That being so I wish I could be more helpful about your Orde problem. But
I'm not sure I can. Louis did indeed create a Dutch "nobility" on Feb.
14th 1807 but it was called the Orde van de Unie not, as far as I know
van de Nederlandse Leeuw. On the other hand the latter, as a Lodewijkian
creation does ring a faint and tiny carillon at the far recesses of my
memory but I'm damned if I can find any ref. to it. Have you tried
the relevant Colenbrander narrative or the Gedenkstukken for Feb 1807
or the NNjBk, or pthe absurd Labarre de Raillicourt which is very
strong on flummery.

    Now, as for the Orde van de Unie, my Almanac de Cour for 1809
has it divided into three orders, the Grand Croix which goes to men of
Ministerraad rank or Rd van Staat as well as to top foreign ministers
(Mollien), princes of Empire, allies etc. Below that comes Commanders
including other Raden van Staat (Appelius, Calkoen) as well as high
nobs and notables like Anthony Hope, and Hodshon the Amsterdam banker-
schepen; Pieter van de Kasteele and others of that ilk. The Commanders
including people like Gerrit Pijman and Robert Voute seem to me to be
partly comprised of a list drwan up by Gogel and another by Mollerus
in 1807, but the King was constantly asking for nominees, meeting them
and making upx his own mind. Third comes the Ridderschap (Chevaliers)
of which in 1809 I make there to be roughly some 7-800! *** Nearly all
native Dutch and obviously (and erroneously) including anyone who could
be thought to be a friend of the regime or a man worthy of note or
open to persuasion by flattery - thus it includes a Bentinck van
Diepenheim (how optimistic can you get) as well as Johannes Busch, Anton
Falck, Carel Hultman, Reuvens the jurist - virtually all the high elite of
the old nobility, Torck van Rosendaels; a van Schuylenburg van
Bommende, the van Hasselts and so on. No Indonesian potentates though.
It seems likely that the Ridderschap got made up on the same basis as the
Napoleonic amalgame - of restoring the trad. nobility and supplementing
them with those whom the King wished to honour. More than that I can't
say. Commanders and Grand Crossers had the right to nominate I think
so perhaps an award of another Orde was within Daendels's purlieu - he
was a holder of the Grand Cross of course along with Gogel and
Schimmelpenninck. But it's odd that in the official Almanac there shouldn't
be a mention of the Leeuw, isn't it. I should perhaps add that none of the
imperial gazettes will be much good since N. refused to acknowledge that
Louis had any right to create or reaffirm such ranks and a condition of
the limited partition of March-April 1810 was that it was abolished.

    Sorry I can't be more helpful. Have you tried writing the IIde
Afdeeling or even the KB?

    See you soon I hope - I'm off to the States for a bit,

                                    Ever,
                                    [Signed:] Simon
```
