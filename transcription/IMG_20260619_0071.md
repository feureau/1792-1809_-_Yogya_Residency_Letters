As you know I really<br>didn't expect to get the job so it all came as an extraordinary<br>surprise. I'm hugely looking forward to coming to Oxford even though<br>I'm fully briefed as to the agonies as well as the ecstasies. I hear<br>from Richard that congrats are in order for you too for getting that<br>plum award from the BA to go East. Lucky s.o.b. - I'm green with envy<br>at all that lovely research time. Roll on the first sabbatical.<br><br>I do feel terrible about that balls-up I made in Cambridge -<br>how can you ever forgive me. I was so discombobulated what with the job<br>news and the sudden and rather dramatic crisis it had plunged Christ's<br>history into that everything else got swept clean away - milles salaams.<br>That being so I wish I could be more helpful about your Orde problem. But<br>I'm not sure I can. Louis did indeed create a Dutch "nobility" on Feb.<br>14th 1807 but it was called the Orde van de Unie not, as far as I know<br>van de Nederlandse Leeuw. On the other hand the latter, as a Lodewijkian<br>creation does ring a faint and tiny carillon at the far recesses of my<br>memory but I'm damned if I can find any ref. to it. Have you tried<br>the relevant Colenbrander narrative or the Gedenkstukken for Feb 1807<br>or the NNjBk, or pthe absurd Labarre de Raillicourt which is very<br>strong on flummery.<br><br>Now, as for the Orde van de Unie, my Almanac de Cour for 1809<br>has it divided into three orders, the Grand Croix which goes to men of<br>Ministerraad rank or Rd van Staat as well as to top foreign ministers<br>(Mollien), princes of Empire, allies etc. Below that comes Commanders<br>including other Raden van Staat (Appelius, Calkoen) as well as high<br>nobs and notables like Anthony Hope, and Hodshon the Amsterdam banker-<br>schepen; Pieter van de Kasteele and others of that ilk. The Commanders<br>including people like Gerrit Pijman and Robert Voute seem to me to be<br>partly comprised of a list drwan up by Gogel and another by Mollerus<br>in 1807, but the King was constantly asking for nominees, meeting them<br>and making upx his own mind. Third comes the Ridderschap (Chevaliers)<br>of which in 1809 I make there to be roughly some 7-800! *** Nearly all<br>native Dutch and obviously (and erroneously) including anyone who could<br>be thought to be a friend of the regime or a man worthy of note or<br>open to persuasion by flattery - thus it includes a Bentinck van<br>Diepenheim (how optimistic can you get) as well as Johannes Busch, Anton<br>Falck, Carel Hultman, Reuvens the jurist - virtually all the high elite of<br>the old nobility, Torck van Rosendaels; a van Schuylenburg van<br>Bommende, the van Hasselts and so on. No Indonesian potentates though.<br>It seems likely that the Ridderschap got made up on the same basis as the<br>Napoleonic amalgame - of restoring the trad. nobility and supplementing<br>them with those whom the King wished to honour. More than that I can't<br>say. Commanders and Grand Crossers had the right to nominate I think<br>so perhaps an award of another Orde was within Daendels's purlieu - he<br>was a holder of the Grand Cross of course along with Gogel and<br>Schimmelpenninck. But it's odd that in the official Almanac there shouldn't<br>be a mention of the Leeuw, isn't it. I should perhaps add that none of the<br>imperial gazettes will be much good since N. refused to acknowledge that<br>Louis had any right to create or reaffirm such ranks and a condition of<br>the limited partition of March-April 1810 was that it was abolished.<br><br>Sorry I can't be more helpful. Have you tried writing the IIde<br>Afdeeling or even the KB?<br><br>See you soon I hope - I'm off to the States for a bit,<br><br>Ever,<br>                                    [Signed:] Simon<br>``` |
