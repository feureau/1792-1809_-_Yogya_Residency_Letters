## Archival & Historical Notes

- **Tobacco Toll Taxes (4 November 1803):**
  - Tollgate tariffs on tobacco set at 2 dubbeltjes (formerly 2 duits) per pikul in Kêdhu, compared to 3 dubbeltjes per pikul in Mataram proper.
  - Taxes per pikul increased by 1 to 2 dubbeltjes across Kêdhu tollgates.
- **Raden Ngabèhi Sasrawijaya & Court Kinship:**
  - Raden Ngabèhi Sasrawijaya (Bupati of Padangan / later Pacitan; appointed Patih to Raden Rangga Prawiradirja III and killed during the Rangga rebellion on 17 October 1810).
  - His wife, Raden Ayu Sasrawijaya, was the elder sister of Ratu Agêng Tegalreja; by generational protocol, Sultan Hamengkubuwana II was obliged to address her honorifically as "mother" (*ibu*).
- **The Malangnegara Lineage & Royal Immunity:**
  - Mas Tumenggung Jumanagara and Mas Ngabèhi Jumadirja are grandsons of **Malangnegara**, the famed Balinese military commander who loyally served Hamengkubuwana I throughout the Giyanti War (Third Javanese War of Succession, 1749–1755).
  - HB I had sanctified this devotion with a solemn royal decree: *"The blood of Malangnegara is the same as my blood"* (cited in Ann Kumar, *Surapati: Man and Legend*, p. 153).
  - Because of this ancestral inviolability, it proved virtually impossible for Dutch authorities to secure punishment against Malangnegara's descendants—even when directly implicated in murdering Europeans in Kêdhu with their own hands or inciting banditry.
  - Furthermore, Mas Ngabèhi Jumadirja's sister was Sultan HB II's primary royal concubine, **Raden Ayu Nêpuk**, whose offspring were deeply cherished by the Sultan. Consequently, HB II chose to pardon and protect relatives involved in robbery incitement (such as Sumanasara, Utandi, and Sumadirja, who were noted for their handsome appearance at the Yogyakarta court).
- **Royal Mourning:** HB II slowly emerges from his acute grief over Ratu Agêng's passing and retreats to his country estate at **Ngawatreja**.
