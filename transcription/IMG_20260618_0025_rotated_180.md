### Pacitan Conditions, Transport, Taxation, and Governance (1755–1793)

* **Distance & Geography:** 40 hours journey East of Yogya; heavy transport difficulties. Smuggling cannot be entirely prevented.
* **Inhabitants:** Pacitan population less disciplined; must be handled more carefully and gently than in Loano/Winong.
* **Commerce:** River export possible; population accustomed to pepper cultivation. Price: 2–2 1/2 silver Rixdollars (*Sp. D.*) per pikul.
* **Landholdings:** 200 cacahs / 48 jungs sawah in Pacitan (Yogya) + 200 cacahs (Solo). Ringed by mountains; roads often impassable. Chinese tollgate keepers warned against smuggling.
* **Agricultural Expansion:** Sawah area in Pacitan greatly expanded since Giyanti Peace of 1755, but extra land was concealed from court by local farmers.
* **Economic Prosperity:** Regent reports local inhabitants make a good living with corvée remitted; more prosperous since Giyanti Partition than under Kartasura.
* **Local Administration:** Regent Nitrawijaya (now Wongsocitro) and his son take advantage of remote distance from court. Population would flee if handled roughly.
* **Supervision:** Resident instructed to monitor Regent to prevent oppression. *Nuwala* issued raising landholding from 6 to 12 jungs to satisfy father/son while retaining labor obligations for pepper.
* **Loano:** 26 jungs could all be planted with pepper.
