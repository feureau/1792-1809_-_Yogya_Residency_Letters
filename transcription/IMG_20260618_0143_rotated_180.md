## Archival & Historical Notes

- **Waterloo's Inaugural Gifts & Equine Protocol (26 July 1803):**
  - Incoming Resident J.G. Waterloo presents ceremonial gifts to Sultan Hamengkubuwana II: an official carriage, an elephant, and a prized Persian stallion. Outgoing Resident Van den Berg adds a Sumbawa/Bima pony (*Bimanese horse*) prior to departing for Semarang.
  - Strict kraton sumptuary and security rules: ordinary Javanese subjects are barred from entering the Sultan's royal stables, and even Patih Danureja II is prohibited from owning Bimanese horses (a royal prerogative).
- **Administration of Grobogan (3 August 1803):** HB II appoints Raden Tumenggung Sasrakusuma as Second Bupati in Grobogan, placed under the overarching authority of Raden Rangga Prawiradirja III (Bupati Wedana of Eastern Mancanagara).
- **Linguistic Deficits at the Residency (7 August 1803):** Waterloo reports that the Yogyakarta Residency completely lacks a competent Javanese translator/interpreter when Patih Danureja II arrives to confer on state affairs.
- **Court Festivities, Expenses & Klaten Fort (17 August 1803):**
  - HB II emerges from the inner palace to preside over the royal tournament (*senenan* / jousting games).
  - Waterloo complains of the heavy financial burden of hosting reciprocal banquets for Javanese nobles during feast days.
  - Notes that Resident Van den Berg in Surakarta is "50x busier than Waterloo."
  - The border fortress at Klaten (*Fort Moerbon* / Marbon) remains under construction; Van den Berg travels from Surakarta to inspect work on the site.
- **Engelhard's High-Level Diplomacy:** A previous misunderstanding (*afscheid*) between Governor Engelhard, the Crown Prince, and HB II is amicably smoothed over. Dutch carpenters arrive to carry out construction/repairs on colonial government buildings following consultations with the Patih and regents.
