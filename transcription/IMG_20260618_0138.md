[Red vertical bar: \|]<br>```<br><br>## Archival & Historical Notes<br><br>- **Pacitan Coastal Interaction:** Continues the account of the Balinese/Bugis fleet at Segarawedi: 3 water buffaloes bartered for 200 Spanish Reals' worth of benzoin, ginger, iron, and steel. The visitors spent nine days on shore enjoying cockfights by day and *tadhak* dances by night, while local Javanese strictly concealed the routes into the interior.<br>- **Sultan's Coastal Defense (19 Apr 1805):** HB II dispatches two 3-pounder cannons and 24 cannonballs to bolster the defenses of Pacitan harbor.<br>- **Pirates Penetrating the Interior & Clashes in East Java (26 Apr 1805):** Reports from Surakarta confirm that raiders landing at Segarawedi struck far inland, penetrating toward Malang. On 29 Besar AJ 1729, combined forces of the Regents of Pasuruan, Bangil, and Malang engaged them in pitched battle at Desa Gondanglegi (Malang), where the Patih of Malang was wounded.<br>- **Deposition of Escapee Rêksamênta (4–8 May 1805):**<br>  - Deposition taken at Yogyakarta from Rêksamênta of Puan Putra Pinang (Cirebon), who had been abducted on Java's north coast and held captive for two months before escaping at Pulau Sempu (off Malang coast).<br>  - Describes an armada of 19 ships, each carrying 20 men, two 6-pounder cannons, plus muskets and blunderbusses (*donderbussen*).<br>  - Identifies leaders as Malay-speaking chiefs from Timor named Raja Datuk Samat and Raja Usus.<br>  - The fleet raided Cilacap, capturing local inhabitants and seizing two cannons, before Rêksamênta leaped overboard and swam ashore at Segarawedi.<br>- **Purwadipura's Field Intelligence to Sumadiningrat:**<br>  - Raden Tumenggung Purwadipura reports on atrocities committed at Cilacap: four local Demangs (Jagayuda, Martarana, Suramênggala, Kartajaya) were attacked and two were beheaded.<br>  - The raiding confederation numbered 35 vessels carrying 50–60 men each (totaling 1,750–2,100 men), armed with 4 large guns and 20 smaller cannon, identified as Bugis and Timorese under Raja Datuk Samat, Raja Usus, Patih Due Rapue, and a Chinese convert named Encik Putih.<br>  - While anchored at Desa Prigi (Trenggalek coast), they held nightly *nayub* dancing and daytime cockfighting. |
