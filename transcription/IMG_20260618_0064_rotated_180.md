### [Transcription]

#### 1. Peasant Demonstration & Open Revolt in Kaliwungu

- Severe social unrest and open demonstrations by local inhabitants against their Bupati, **Raden Tumenggung Natadiningrat**.  
- Districts of **Gênḍing** and **Pakis** enter into open revolt.  
- Translator for the Javanese Language, **H.D. van den Berg**, dispatched to investigate the grievances.

#### 2. Charges of Tyranny, Extortion, and Abuses

- Arbitrary dismissals: Bupati dismissed large numbers of traditional *dêmang*, *mantri*, *lurah*, and village officials without cause.  
- Offices sold to highest bidders (*places given to those who pay Bupati the most*), staffing the administration with his personal sycophants (*entourage*).  
- Extortion: Accepted bribe money from aspirants seeking appointments, but kept the funds without delivering the promised offices.  
- Personal debauchery: Accused of sleeping with and debauching the wives of his subjects.  
- Excessive taxation & land alienation: Increased corvée burdens and curtailed peasants' ancestral rice fields in order to lease the roadside sawahs to external tenants.  
- Treated senior elders and officials with contempt and arrogance.  
- **Outcome:** Bupati confesses his abuses; summarily dismissed by the Dutch government.

#### 3. Dynastic Legitimacy & Prophecy of Sultan Agung

- The population demands a replacement Bupati drawn strictly from the ancient lineage that had governed Kaliwungu for two centuries.  
- Reference to ancestral legitimation:  
  Sultan Agung had prophesied upon the installation of Kaliwungu's first Bupati, **Sumadiwirya** (a son of Pangeran Lêmpuyangan), that none other than direct descendants of Sumadiwirya should ever rule Kaliwungu, resting under the ancestral protection of the sacred shrine of **Batara Katong**.
