### [Translation of the Gold Farm Charter (*Piagem*) Granted by HB II to R.T. Reksanegara — Page 1]

<u>Translation of the Gold Farm letter given by H.H. Sultan Hamëngkubuwana II, residing at Yogyakarta Adiningrat on the island of Java</u>

I Sultan Hamëngkubuwana etc give this letter under my seal to my subject Reksanegara as a proof (evidence) to all my subjects both Bupatis and Mantris who come under my rule in Yogyakarta Adiningrat as well as those in the mancanagara provinces, to make known that the reason why I have accorded this letter to Reksanegara is because I have once again favoured him and hereby permitted him to take the farms of all domains such as bandars or tollgates rangkahs, salarangs, the gedhong tembakau [^1] in Kedhu, the slaughtering of buffaloes, the salterns (salt works) on the south coast and the birds' nest rocks; everything which is under my administration in Yogyakarta Adiningrat for an amount of 60,000 ronde realen silver per annum and in case payment be made in copper duits, the same shall be acceptable at the rate of ~~28 dubbeltjes of 10 duits each~~ 1 real for 28 dubbeltjes of 10 duits each. The tax farm money must be paid in two installments each year: half or 30,000 r.r. at the festival of Mulud and the other half, also 30,000 at the feast of Puasa. Under this tax farm are not to be included my pasars and rangkahs withing the capital (Yogya) as well as those in the vicinity, these being also excepted such as Tanjung Tirto, Tegalsari (with its tollgate) and the so-called pasar perdikan which belong to the priests as well as my pasars and *further that the* bandars in the mancanagara provinces as well as the tollgates along the great river (Bengawana Sala) and the birds nest rocks in those areas be also excepted from the same---- furthermore if the tobacco harvest has not been placed in the warehouses and the time of this farm be expired, this article will be considered to relate to this and not to *the* subsequent farm (similarly with the three collections of birds nests each year) -- the farm of the birds nests is worth 12000 r.r. for each half year, but this is to be separate and not to be reckoned under the previous tax returns, and it is also stipulated that I shall be allowed to send *down* for my own use 110 <u>sapi pengluput</u> or beasts of burden three times each year without having to pay tolls or other taxes on passing though tollgates, <u>rangkahs</u> and <u>salarangs</u>.

On each occasion that the farm moneys are paid, the <u>pangirings</u> or presents must be: <u>pathoolen (kains)</u>; (see n. of Rouffaer), Gold thread, silk, tea, candy, sugar and Chinese sweetmeats (confituuren), candied fruit; consisting of 10 pairs Pathoolen Jelamprang, each pr. worth 25 r.r.; 10 pathoolen kanigara, each pr. worth 15 r.r.; if I want pathoolen kenanga people must produce them for me at the same price as the pathoolen jelamprang and kanigara; 10 pounds of Gold thread each of 12 threads and 18 heavy reals ? worth 24 reals; 20 catties of silk, of which 9 catties of green and 9 of red and 2 of rose (merah jambu) or light red silk, the cattie I fix at 8 reals worth. These together are worth the sum of 1230 r.r. under which is reckoned the sum of 430 r.r. per half year for the payment of candy sugar, tea and candied fruit (confituren); the patompos money is reckoned *out* at 10 per 1000 (1 % of the instalment), 30 r.r. for the purchase of the serat papak and 31 r.r. for the panglamar or accountant (? goldsmith) or Sultan's goldsmith (?)

If there are some of my subjects who pass tolls carrying loads (pikulans) which do not contain merchandise (negotiable goods), I permit them to pass without paying a toll -- if there are any of my family, children or great or small priyayis who possess ricefields and they transport the padi or rice to Yogya either by coolies or on horseback or on other beasts of burden they are also to be allowed to pass through (without paying tolls) - whenever one of my subjects at the time of the Garebeg feast journeys here or even outside the time of the Garebeg abd they carry loads with/out negotiable goods (merchandise) I do not allow it that tolls should be raised on them but if the load is asked to be inspected at the tollgate this must be allowed -- and if they are found to carry merchandise they must pay the due toll for it, but they must pass through freely if they have no merchandise; people should make a distinction between merchandise and non-merchandise and I forbid that tolls be raised on small quantities of kains and tobacco which are being carried as presents for their llords apanage holders.
