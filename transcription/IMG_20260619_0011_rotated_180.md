### Land Tenure Structure, Village Sawahs, and Labor Systems (1806)

* **Land Ownership:**
  * Most village inhabitants do **not** own their sawahs; all land divided into cacahs owned by the Sultan.
  * Exceptions: Mountain dwellers, *Blandhong* teak forest laborers, and road coolies employed by Chinese/Arab traders.
  * *V.O.C. Strategy:* As native courts gain economic and political strength over time, the Dutch should bide their time in asserting authority over Solo and Yogya.
* **Land Tenure & Sawah Division:**
  * Population density varies widely: 50 men live on 1 jung of land in some districts, vs. 2–4 men in others. Jung dimensions vary between Kabupatens.
  * Sawahs split in heterogeneous, irregular plots, but each farmer recognizes his own plot.
  * **Tenure Security:** Grandsons retain possession of lands held by grandfathers; land transfers rare unless village *Lurah/Bekel* reassigns plots.
* **Village Population Classes:**
  * *Orang Menumpang:* Landless villagers residing in desas who perform corvée services for landholders.
  * Most royal *Prajurits* own no sawahs, though their total number remains small.
