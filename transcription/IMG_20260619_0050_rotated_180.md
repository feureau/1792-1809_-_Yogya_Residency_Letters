### Hydrographic Surveys, Coinage Economies, & Surakarta Financial Crisis (1806–1808)

* **Residency Command Transfer (22 June 1808):** Instructions issued to Lt.-Col. Reinking to manage the Residency during Acting Resident Van Braam's absence.
* **Coastal Survey Suspension (21 Sept. 1806):** Marine School Instructor Lt. Kortsino and Cadet Du Bois forced to suspend coastal surveys from Mancingan to Pacitan due to hazardous rocky reefs.
* **Coinage Economies & Fiscal Deficits (1 Nov. 1806 - Liebeherr to Engelhard):**
  * Minting reduced by 200,000 Rijksdaalders annually; replaced with paper/copper currency to prevent fiscal confusion.
  * Impossible to reduce court expenses in Surakarta. Resident Liebeherr suffers heavy personal losses due to European war debts; paid only 15,000 paper p.a., forcing him to assume direct administration of Sunan's Tax-Farm Domains.
  * *Domain Profits:* Only birds' nest harvests yield net profits (court payments required strictly in silver money). Opium monopoly unprofitable as Chinese and Javanese consumers are too impoverished.
  * V.O.C. must maintain *Strandgeld* payments to keep the Surakarta garrison ready to suppress internal uprisings.
* **Naval Threats & Defense Measures (Dec. 1806):**
  * British warships attack Dutch shipping in Batavia Bay; PB IV and Sunan warned to prepare defenses. Liebeherr refuses to march 1/2 the Solo garrison to Semarang without direct orders from Engelhard.
  * PB IV proposes establishing a civil guard of prominent citizens to defend the Kraton and Fort.
