## Archival & Historical Notes

- **Source Context:** Extracts from Governor Nicolaas Engelhard's official tour journal (*Verslag der reis van N. Engelhard naar de Hoven...*, Semarang, 27 May 1803), published in J.K.J. de Jonge, *De Opkomst van het Nederlandsch Gezag in Oost-Indië*, vol. 13, pp. 118–283.
- **The Klaten Fort:** Mentions the erection of the fort between Surakarta and Yogyakarta at "Marbon" / Klaten.
- **24 August 1802 Yogya Conference:**
  - Attendees: Sultan Hamengkubuwana II, Resident van den Berg, Crown Prince (Pangeran Adipati Anom, later HB III), and Patih Danureja II.
  - Primary Dutch aims: Guaranteeing military troop support in the event of an English invasion (Napoleonic Wars), and settling financial installments celebrating Johannes Siberg's inauguration as Governor-General.
- **Dutch Grievances & Protocol Demands:**
  - Surveillance of Chinese *peranakan* settlers in the Tersono district, who had caused friction for Resident van Leyden.
  - Outrage that Dutch Residents were frequently made to wait 3 to 4 months before being granted royal audiences by HB II.
  - Strict insistence that all administrative matters pass through the Patih (*Rijksbestierder*) to avoid royal stonewalling.
  - Danureja II formally takes his oath of fidelity to the Dutch East India Company / Batavian Republic.
- **Infrastructure & Agricultural Demands:**
  - Cultivation of pepper and indigo tied to the urgent maintenance of bridges and transit roads (noting specific annoyance at the dilapidated Boyolali road due to the Sunan's failure to supply road repair materials).
- **Kraton Power Dynamics:**
  - Identifies Raden Tumenggung Judhadaningrat / Sumadiningrat (the Sultan's favorite son-in-law and military commander) as the true "key man in kraton" through whom all vital court business was steered.
  - HB II embraced Engelhard with diplomatic cordiality, urging past tensions to be forgotten, though Engelhard recognized the deep dissembling at court.
- **Character Profile of the Crown Prince (HB III):**
  - Sharply contrasted with HB II: approachable, sociable, expressive, and conversational. Engelhard notes with anticipation whether he will emulate the proud autocracy of his father (HB II) or the statesmanlike wisdom of his grandfather (HB I) once he accedes to the throne.
