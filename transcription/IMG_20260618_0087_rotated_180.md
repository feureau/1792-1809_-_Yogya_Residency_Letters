Visit to Yogya cancelled.**<br>- `N.O.K. 47`<br>- `MvO Van Reede - 3.`<br><br>### Nederburgh's Visit Cancelled & HB II [Sultan Hamengkubuwana II]'s Defiance:<br><br>- HB II persists in his outright refusal to accord Commissioner-General Sebastiaan Cornelis Nederburgh any greater ceremonial respect than that accorded to an ordinary Governor of Java's Northeast Coast.<br>  - Although HB II conceded he would meet him at the valley of Téam [?] and send the Crown Prince to Kalasan.<br>  - HB II argued there was no stipulation in the treaties and contracts with Yogyakarta compelling him to receive such a visit.<br>- **National / Divine mission:** HB II declared that if Nederburgh visited Surakarta without visiting Yogyakarta, he would treat it as an intolerable insult, proclaiming:<br>  - **\*** *Says that God had chosen him "to uphold the Honour of the Javanese Nation".* **\*** [red margin bar and underline].<br><br>### The Sultan's Blood-Brotherhood Pact & War Preparations:<br><br>- In a secret conference with his royal brothers, HB II secures their absolute loyalty to maintain a united front:<br>  - In a solemn traditional ritual, HB II **makes rice balls with his own hand**, which all the brothers eat together in covenant.<br>  - Danureja I also assisted at this conference (dating it prior to August 1799, c. 1796–1799).<br>- **Raden Panji Padnawijaya:** Royal Treasurer of HB II; directed to produce funds.<br>- Royal bodyguards (*Lifewatch*) swear a new oath of fealty to HB II.<br>- Treasury funds prepared to purchase arms and ammunition for the Sultan's *prajurit* regiments.<br><br>### Diplomatic Outcome & Feudal Vocabulary Dispute:<br><br>- Nederburgh is forced to postpone his visit to both Yogyakarta and Surakarta.<br>- Instead, the Patih is sent to Semarang.<br>- **Linguistic dispute over Sovereignty:** Van Reede observes that the feudal Dutch terms *"Leen"* (fief) and *"Leenheer"* (feudal overlord) do not occur in the official Javanese translations of the contracts.<br>  - Nicolaas Hartingh spoke and wrote excellent Javanese and would never have let such words slip by unintentionally; questions whether the text was modified in the Yogyakarta Kraton copy.<br>- In 1798, Governor Van Overstraten advised Resident Van IJsseldijk upon his return from Batavia to obtain military reinforcements for the garrisons in Surakarta and Yogyakarta. |
