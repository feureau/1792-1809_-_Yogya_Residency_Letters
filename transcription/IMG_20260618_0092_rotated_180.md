### Text & Marginal Annotations

[The Faction of Demang Rangga Jajar (Serang / Kadilangu):]

- The elderly leader had recently died.
- **Sons and Grandsons of Demang Rangga Jajar:**
  - **Sons:** Eldest = Demang Rangga; Raden Purbakusuma; Raden Anrangkusuma; Raden Sasradipura.
  - **Grandsons:** Raden Nastrawijaya; Raden Sumadirana.
  - Mostly dwell in the wooden villages (*desas kayu*) around Surakarta.
  - **Insurrectionary threat:** Assembling up to 1,000 men to attack Dutch V.O.C. outposts from the landward side if the British launch an attack from the sea.
  - Governor Van Overstraten wanted the Radèns captured and imprisoned, following discussions with the Demak Bupati, Patih, and Nayakas at Surakarta.
  - However, the authorities succeeded in persuading them to come peacefully to Surakarta of their own free will; Raden Rangga was given an official court position in Surakarta.

---

[Regent of Tegal & Bali Exile (f. 160):]

- **Bp. of Tegal Raden Maji flees to Bali** [underlined in red].
  - Later apprehended and sentenced to exile at the Cape of Good Hope, where he died.
  - Left an outstanding debt to Gusti of Karangasem (Bali) amounting to **2,000 Sp.D.**
  - The wife of the Bupati of Tegal (sister of the Panembahan of Madura) is currently still residing in Bali [red margin bar].

---

[Agriculture & Cotton Production (f. 196):]

- Very poor harvests of *kapas* (raw cotton) in Java.
- Cannot withstand foreign competition; best cotton seeds can be obtained from Coromandel and Makassar, where cotton flourishes exceedingly well in Java.

---

[Textiles for Company Slaves (f. 197):]

- Javanese cloth woven and supplied for Company slaves in Batavia.
- Contracted and made up by the Kapitan Cina in Yogyakarta: **350 *korgē* (corge = 20 pieces) per annum @ 16.32 Sp.D.**
- By 1788, the volume was too large for Yogyakarta alone, so half the quota was transferred to Surakarta.
