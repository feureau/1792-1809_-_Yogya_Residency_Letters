## Archival & Historical Notes

- **Crown Prince's Diplomatic Charm:** The Crown Prince (later HB III) summons Resident Waterloo and proves completely charming, effusively offering professions of steadfast friendship toward the Dutch East India Company / Batavian Republic.
- **Geopolitical News (26 October 1803):** Governor Engelhard informs Waterloo of the **Peace of Amiens** between England and France, expressing anxiety that the Batavian Republic would inevitably be drawn into future hostilities.
- **Burial of Ratu Agêng Tegalreja at Imagiri (29 October 1803):**
  - Ratu Agêng is conveyed to the royal necropolis at **Imagiri**, four hours' journey south of Yogyakarta.
  - Sultan Hamengkubuwana II and the Crown Prince remain behind in the capital, both visibly overcome with grief, while all other court nobles follow the funeral cortege to Imagiri.
- **Ratu Agêng's Dying Exhortation to Sultan HB II (28 October 1803):**
  - Recorded in contemporary Dutch translation from Javanese:
    > *"Sultan: De weg die ik af te leggen heb is zwaar, en nu gevoel ik! Niets meer dan de gemeente mens te weezen, houd dit mij zoon voor oogen en gelooft niet, dat ofschoon uw nu Vorst zijt, dat uw na u dood iets meer is dan als een gemeen Batoor, en weest daar na! —"*  
    > (*"Sultan: The path that I must tread is heavy, and now I feel it! To be nothing more than common mankind. Keep this before your eyes, my son, and do not believe that, although you are now a Sovereign, after your death you are anything more than a common servant (batoor) — conduct yourself accordingly!"*)  

  - The dying matriarch's solemn rebuke made a profound emotional impression on the proud Sultan.
- **Factional Sabotage of Gabriel Eenst Murder Inquiry (31 October 1803):**
  - Raden Tumenggung Sumadiningrat's factional animosity against Patih Danureja II and the Outer Regents (*Bupati Nayaka Jawi*) impedes and obstructs the investigation into the robbery and murder of the European timber trader Gabriel Eenst in Grobogan/Jipang.
