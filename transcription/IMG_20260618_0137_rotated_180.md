## Archival & Historical Notes

- **Teak Extraction & Security in Mancanagara (March 1803):**
  - Governor Engelhard requests permission to fell teak timber in the Sultan's forests, granted subject to strict stipulations.
  - A punitive expedition against bandit gangs in Mancanagara returns bringing severed ears of slain robbers and numerous captives.
  - Patih Danureja II and Resident Van den Berg discuss timber quotas from Jipang to coastal districts (Rembang to Sidayu); timber extraction is restricted to HB II's own coastal subjects, with Chinese and coastal outsiders barred from entering the interior regencies without explicit Dutch passes.
- **The Unmeasured Waste Lands (*Tanah Baon*) Dispute (31 Jan 1804):**
  - Resident Waterloo inquires whether the colonial administration should survey unmeasured/waste lands belonging to the Courts, which were omitted from formal royal *cacah* (household) registers.
  - HB II consistently blocked colonial land surveys, referring back to his 3 April 1792 conference with Van Overstraten.
  - Comparative census figures noted:
    - 1755 Treaty of Giyanti: Sunan PB III (85,450 *cacahs*), Sultan HB I (87,050 *cacahs*).
    - 1773 Census: both rulers registered over 100,000 *cacahs*.
  - HB II feared that if the 1773 registers were taken as a fixed ceiling, any newly cultivated or reclaimed land (such as vast new sawahs cleared behind Lamongan, Juwana, and especially Grobogan) would be expropriated by the Dutch government.
- **Maritime Incursions & Piracy along Java's South Coast (1804–1805):**
  - 5 March 1804: 80–90 Mandarese / Balinese pirates from Buleleng operating off Prigi on the South Coast of Java.
  - 1 April 1804: News received of the murder of the Sultan of Banten.
  - 17 April 1805 (Pacitan Landing): A massive fleet of 32 Balinese and Bugis vessels carrying 400 armed men drops anchor at Segarawedi in the Pacitan area (Surakarta enclave). 17 vessels anchor offshore while landing parties come ashore.
  - Yogya dispatches Raden Tumenggung Purwadipura and 15 *mantris* of the *Gêdhong Têngên* to Pacitan to investigate.
  - Detailed bartering economics between local coastal villagers and the seaborne landing party: ginger, cosmetic paste (*boreh*), and incense (*benzoin* / kemenyan) offered as gifts; chickens sold for ½ Rupiah, hens for 1 Real; 1 *bakul* of rice exchanged for a firearm, and a *tompo* of rice traded for 3 beakers of ginger.
