## Archival & Historical Notes

- **Raden Rangga Prawiradirja III's Arrears (12 August 1803):** Resident Waterloo severely reprimands Raden Rangga Prawiradirja III because his tax farm payments from Eastern Mancanagara are 16 months in arrears, amounting to 1,300 silver *ronde maaten*.
- **Kraton Financial Parsimony & Marriage Stalemate (20 Aug – 4 Sept 1803):**
  - Resident Waterloo and Second Resident Domis visit Sultan HB II, the official queens (*Ratus*), and the Crown Prince.
  - Waterloo comments sharply on the Sultan's extreme parsimony and financial obstinacy (*"verregaande inzadeligheid"* / *karigheid* in money matters); the Crown Prince is completely powerless to intervene.
  - Negotiations for the prospective dynastic marriage between the courts of Yogyakarta and Surakarta remain stalled without fresh news.
- **Catastrophic Earthquake of 7 September 1803:**
  - At 9:57 AM (three minutes before ten) on 7 September 1803, an exceptionally violent earthquake strikes Yogyakarta, acknowledged by the elderly as the most destructive tremor in living memory.
  - Four major shocks rock the region over a 17-day period.
  - **Severe Physical Damage:**
    - Extensive damage throughout the Kraton and the **Taman Sari** water palace.
    - The Sultan's newly built country retreat at **Ngawatreja** is heavily damaged.
    - The Chinese temple / pavilion at Taman Sari is completely wrecked, and the hydraulic seals of the pleasure pools fracture, causing all water to drain out.
    - The northwest bulwark of the Dutch garrison fort (Fort Rustenburg / Vredeburg) cracks and suffers structural damage.
- **Rupture between HB II and Raden Rangga Prawiradirja III (13 September 1803):**
  - A major personal and political dispute erupts after Rangga illegally confiscates appanage land previously granted in perpetuity to one of the Sultan's court favorites.
  - In retaliation, HB II orders Rangga's principal wife, the royal princess **Ratu Maduretna** (HB II's daughter), summoned back from Maospati/Madiun to live inside the Kraton, while Rangga's mother-in-law is excluded from palace affairs.
  - Fearing Rangga's licentious behavior and infidelity in her absence, HB II subsequently summons Rangga's concubines and the Ratu, an overture interpreted by Waterloo as a sign of imminent reconciliation.
