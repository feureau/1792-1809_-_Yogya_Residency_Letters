| **October 1796** | ### [Transcription]<br><br>#### 1. Demak & Adilangu<br><br>- *[Red underline:]* **Dêmak Area & Adilangu:**  <br>  Noted as a place of refuge / sanctuary for many Javanese fugitives and criminals.<br><br>- Ruled by **Pangeran Wijil**:  <br>  Old Pangeran died in 1784; succeeded by his son.  <br>  Described as a very fine, brave Javanese gentleman from an ancient and eminent family; recommended to be given a regular Regency when a vacancy occurs.<br><br>- Pays an annual tribute to the V.O.C. of **6 koyangs of rice**.<br><br>#### 2. Japara, Kudus & Pati<br><br>- **Declining timber yield in Japara:**  <br>  Production originally fixed at 2,000 teak planks, but recently cut by 500 planks.<br><br>- **Kudus:**  <br>  Very heavily populated district, encompassing the surrounding territory of Japara.<br><br>- Bupati: **R.T. Suradiningrat** (a Madurese), recognized as one of the most diligent and capable Bupatis in the region.  <br>- **Pati:** Administered by two Bupatis:  <br>  - **R.T. Aria Mêgatsari**  <br>  - **R.T. Mangunkusuma** (married to a daughter of Mangkunegara I, by whom he has many children).<br><br>#### 3. Rembang & The Blandong Timber Workers<br><br>- *[Red underline:]* **Rembang:** One of the most sparsely populated areas (*f. 38*).  <br>  - Bupati: **Tumenggung Puspadipura**.  <br>  - Inhabitants earn their livelihood through carpentry and shipbuilding (*timmerwerf*), though work has fallen off considerably in recent years.  <br>  - Forest-cutter (*blandong*) population drawn from **Trambalang**, **Waru**, **Resserman**, and **Morototo**.  <br>  - Required to deliver timber to Rembang yards as well as to the Sunan of Surakarta for state boat-building. |
