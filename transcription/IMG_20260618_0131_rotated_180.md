## Archival & Historical Notes

- **Court Succession Rumors:** Van den Berg / Engelhard record rumors that Pangeran Natakusuma harbored ambitions for the Yogyakarta throne if the Crown Prince (later HB III) were sidelined.
- **Geopolitical Assessment of the Two Courts:** Governor Pieter Gerardus van Overstraten emphasized that Surakarta posed a far greater existential threat to Dutch stability than Yogyakarta. Van Overstraten cites the lingering imperial ambition dating back to the 1790 *Pakêpung* crisis: the Surakarta Sunan (*the Emperor*) had never abandoned the dream of reuniting the divided Mataram empire and subjugating the Sultan of Yogyakarta.
- **Company Anxiety over Elite Intermarriages:** The Dutch government viewed political and marital alliances between the coastal regents (*Bupati Pasisir*, who were direct vassals/administrators of the VOC/Company) and the royal houses of the Vorstenlanden with great alarm. In the event of war between the Dutch and the princes, such marital bonds would split the regents' loyalties.
- **Tragic Fate of Coastal Regents' Daughters in Surakarta:** Coastal bupatis strongly resisted marrying their daughters into the Surakarta court due to abuse and lethal court intrigues. Specifically highlighted: two daughters of the Regent of Pati, Raden Tumenggung Aria Megatsari, who were married into the court (to Pangeran Buminata / Boepati Noto), were mistreated and "done to death" inside the Surakarta Kraton.
