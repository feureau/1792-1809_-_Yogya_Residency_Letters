### Archive Entries: December 1805 (British Invasion Threats & Coastal Observation Posts)

* Reference: **AN 'Djokjo Brieven' No. 20.86 (12 Dec. 1805)**
* **Trengganu Intelligence:** Statements from British merchant captains and Arab trader Narip Husein in Semarang confirm an impending British invasion of Java.
  * British supply vessel arrives from Penang (*Pulo Pinang*) laden with cloth, iron, knives, and cannons.
  * Javanese sailors report 40 British merchantmen and 30 warships at Penang destined for China, with plans for a North Coast landing and interior march in Jan. 1806.
  * British captains report fear of Javanese pikes/krises until assured by Riau/Lingga pirates (who captured 10,000 Javanese) that Javanese scatter at cannon fire.
* **Coastal Observation Posts (16 Dec. 1805 - Waterloo to Engelhard):**
  * Dutch N.C.O.s posted to strategic coastal locations: **Karang Bolong**, **Kadilangu**, **Rawa**, **Mancingan**, **Rongkob**, and **Pacitan** to monitor British ship movements.
  * Waterloo alerts Danureja II to South Coast pirate threats.
  * *Assessment:* Though North Coast surf is rough, British landed at Madras through heavy breakers and could attempt a Java landing.
* **Inter-Court Rivalry Warning:** Memory of 1740 1/2-kingdom division remains fresh; if Dutch are engaged with the British, Pakubuwana IV and Hamengkubuwana II would likely turn on each other rather than assist the Dutch. HB II must be warned of impending danger.
