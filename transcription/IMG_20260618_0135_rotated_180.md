## Archival & Historical Notes

- **PB IV and Nijvenheim Rift:** Sunan Pakubuwana IV believed he had been promised parts of Yogyakarta territory (Mataram) upon the death of Sultan Hamengkubuwana I. Meanwhile, Resident Nijvenheim harbored ambitions to become Governor of Java's Northeast Coast and bad-mouthed Engelhard to the Sunan; PB IV viewed Nijvenheim as having seduced/corrupted Cakrawinata.
- **Surakarta Crown Prince (Later PB V / Sugih) Character Profile:**
  - J.G. van den Berg, after taking leave of HB II and arriving in Surakarta on 18 August 1803, observes extreme hostility between the Crown Prince (future Pakubuwana V) and his uncle Pangeran Mangkubumi.
  - Pangeran Mangkubumi is described as having "a charming heart," tearfully pleading for reconciliation before Van den Berg and addressing the prince respectfully as *Gusti*.
  - The Crown Prince is described scathingly: *"a wraaktzuchtig en haatdragende mannetje"* (a vindictive and rancorous little man) devoid of intelligence, turning his back on his uncle.
  - Carey marks this entry in the margin for use in his article on the **Sepoy Conspiracy of 1815** (where PB V and Surakarta factions were deeply implicated).
- **Court Movements & Marriage Negotiations:**
  - PB IV's leisure trips to Pengging / Boyolali with an armed retinue of 30,000 men (citing Louw, *De Java-Oorlog*, vol. VI, p. 109).
  - Pangeran Prangwedana (Mangkunagaran) confides his grievances against PB IV to Van den Berg.
  - Negotiations for an inter-court marriage between a Yogyakarta princess (Ratu Anom) and the Surakarta Crown Prince; PB IV repeatedly inquires about the bride's age (citing J.W. Winter, *Beknopte Beschrijving van het Hof Soerakarta*, 1843, p. 39).
  - Confirmation that one of the Crown Prince's concubines is pregnant (*"the young Prince begins to look much after the women"*), dispelling longstanding rumors of his impotence.
- **Regional Martial Profiles:** Notes that men from Pajang, Mataram, and Sukawati make good soldiers, whereas troops from Bagêlèn serve mainly as coolies/laborers.
