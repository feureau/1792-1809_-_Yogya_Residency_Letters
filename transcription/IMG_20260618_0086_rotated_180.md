### Main Text & Margin Notes

| Margin Notes | Main Text |
| :--- | :--- |
| **1792 to 1809** | ### Text & Marginal Annotations<br><br>[Pangeran Natakusuma (future Paku Alam I):]<br><br>- **Lineage & Standing:** Through his mother, he descends from the old Kedu dynasty; possesses immense influence in Kedu, holding extensive appanage lands there (cf. Kedu Disturbances 1813).<br>- **Personal Character:** Courteous, gentle/soft character makes him loved by the entire court; acts quietly and honestly; does not push his own ambitions.<br>- **HB I [Sultan Hamengkubuwana I]’s Prophecy:** HB I had prophesied that Natakusuma would one day play an important historical role.<br>- **Support among elite:** Many Princes and Bupatis are secretly attached to him, especially Pangeran Mangkudiningrat, who shows him special respect.<br>- **Rift with HB II [Sultan Hamengkubuwana II]:** HB II had sworn to HB I that he would not diminish or strip anything from Natakusuma’s position. However, shortly after his accession, HB II confiscated some of Natakusuma's ornaments, causing the friendship between Natakusuma and HB II to be broken off.<br><br>[Commissioner-General Nederburgh's Proposed Court Visit (red margin bars & underlines):]<br><br>- Sebastiaan Cornelis Nederburgh visits Semarang and expresses the intention to visit the Principalities courts.<br>- **Postponement:** The planned visit had to be postponed because HB II refused to pay proper diplomatic respects to Nederburgh as Commissioner-General, demanding that Nederburgh be placed on the exact same seating level/ceremonial footing as himself.<br>- **Contrasting responses:**<br>  - Sunan PB IV [Sunan Pakubuwana IV] was prepared to receive Nederburgh with proper ceremonial pomp (*staat*).<br>  - Sultan HB II refused, citing the historical catastrophe that had followed Governor-General Gustaaf Willem van Imhoff’s visit to Surakarta in 1746, which had provoked Pangeran Mangkubumi’s rebellion and flight from the court.<br>  - HB II was therefore entirely unprepared to receive Nederburgh.<br>  - HB II further raised difficulties regarding the 3 or 4 high-ranking Dutch officials who would all have to be seated alongside each other, declaring that this would cause HB II to be deeply shamed in the eyes of his Javanese subjects. |
